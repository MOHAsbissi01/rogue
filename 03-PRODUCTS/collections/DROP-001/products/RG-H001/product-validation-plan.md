# RG-H001 and candidate hoodie validation plan

## Purpose and evidence boundary

FACT: RG-H001 is an existing first prototype intended for testing, not necessarily the final launch product.
Founders identify Costas and report very small orders with no minimum quantity; terms are not independently verified.
PROPOSAL: use that flexibility to explore additional hoodie designs with limited, explicitly authorized sampling.
UNKNOWN: fabric/GSM, final fit/sizes, decoration, measured quality, final costs and customer preference.

## Stage 1 - identify and document the actual prototype

Assign an owner and physical sample ID; confirm whether the existing v01 documentation slot corresponds to its real revision.
Record front, back, details and fit photographs, source artwork, measurement method and observed defects. Preserve originals.
Complete the specification from measurement/supplier evidence; do not infer composition or GSM from a photo.
Agree intended fit, care instructions, measurement points, tolerances, wash cycles and defect severity before testing.
Gate: a dated protocol and sample identity that another reviewer can reproduce, with any test spend authorized.

## Stage 2 - quality, fit and washing

| Area | Proposed method | Evidence to record | Decision rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Construction | Inspect seams, reinforcement, symmetry, cuffs, hem, hood and trims | Detail photos, defect location, severity and reviewer | Revise unresolved critical defects; acceptable tolerances must be agreed first |
| Fit and comfort | Measure consistently and conduct consented fit sessions for intended sizes | Sample size, measurements, movement/comfort notes, participant code | Compare to intended fit; a single sample cannot validate all sizes |
| Wash performance | Use supplier-confirmed care conditions; record detergent, temperature, cycle, drying and number of washes | Matched before/after measurements and photos; distortion, pilling, color and seam observations | Compare to pre-agreed tolerances; unknown care method blocks a meaningful claim |
| Decoration | Inspect print/embroidery before and after wear/wash | Cracking, peeling, puckering, thread loss, placement or color change | Resolve failures before accepting the design/process |
| Repeatability | Compare separately identified samples if authorized | Variation between samples, sizes and batches | Do not infer production consistency from one passing sample |

Measurement change (%) = (after - before) / before x 100, only when the initial measurement is positive.
Keep signed change and units; document rest/conditioning time. These are internal checks, not certified lab results.
No test result, tolerance, care claim or sample approval has been supplied yet.

## Stage 3 - compare designs and gather feedback

PROPOSAL: compare RG-H001 with a small founder-selected set of alternatives, without creating fictional finished products.
Keep base garment, presentation and question order comparable; label drawings/mockups versus real samples clearly.
Use consistent criteria: self-expression/story, graphic appeal, wearability, fit/comfort, workmanship, complexity and fully
quoted landed cost. Record why a design scores differently rather than treating a score as a sales prediction.

Recruit through the [customer research plan](../../../../../01-STRATEGY/customer-research-plan.md). Record anonymized
participant codes, recruitment source, relevant age band and actual response. Rotate presentation order, ask for reasons,
and separate spontaneous preference from willingness to buy at a disclosed provisional price. No fabricated sample size,
positive feedback or demand claim. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal data; obtain consent for photos or quotations.

Gate: both founders choose revise, retest, reject or select a candidate using recorded evidence. Preserve rejected designs
and reasons. If a genuinely new product is chosen, use the product onboarding script and catalog; do not rename RG-H001.

## Stage 4 - controlled small-batch pilot

Confirm Costas' quantity policy, itemized quote, materials, size mix, lead time and defect remedy for the selected design.
Choose quantity only after sample/spec acceptance, rights review, complete order economics and an explicit cash/spend decision.
Record what uncertainty the batch tests, maximum exposure, inspection plan and how unsold/defective items are handled.
Inspect and log every pilot unit where feasible; segregate defects and count actual saleable stock. A purchase does not
mean units are launch-ready. Pilot launch still requires truthful product information, storefront QA and fulfillment readiness.

Stop or revise when critical quality/rights issues are unresolved, costs exceed the approved ceiling, terms are unclear or
feedback reveals an unaddressed fit/design problem. Continue only after actual evidence meets criteria agreed before the test.
Do not scale solely because a supplier allows small orders or social engagement looks encouraging.
